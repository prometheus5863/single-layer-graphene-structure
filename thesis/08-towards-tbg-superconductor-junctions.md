# Chapter 8: Towards Twisted Bilayer Graphene–Superconductor Junctions

*Outline only. This chapter has not been drafted, and no result in it is
claimed. It lists what the registered topic of this thesis needs, what
Chapters 2–6 already supply, and what has to be built.*

## 8.1 The question

- What quantum transport through a twisted bilayer graphene (TBG)–superconductor
  junction looks like, and which parts of it a compact device model can predict.
- Why the single-layer chapters come first: every junction model below starts from
  a Dirac band structure, a gate-controlled carrier density and a metal contact.

## 8.2 What carries over from Chapters 2–6

- Chapter 2: the tight-binding and Dirac-cone description of each layer, and the
  exact-value checks used to validate it.
- Chapter 3: diffusive transport, the minimum conductivity and the disorder floor.
- Chapter 4: quantum capacitance, gate-controlled carrier density, and contact
  doping by a metal (the work-function crossover and its limits).
- Chapter 6: two-contact, signed-carrier treatment of a channel between two
  different contacts.
- Appendix A: the verification method — validate each model against exactly
  known limits before reporting a result.

## 8.3 What is new

- The moiré band structure of TBG (continuum model of Bistritzer and MacDonald,
  *PNAS* 108, 12233 (2011)) and its flat bands near the magic angle.
- Superconducting contacts: Andreev reflection at a graphene–superconductor
  interface, including specular Andreev reflection near the Dirac point
  (Beenakker, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 97, 067007 (2006)).
- The proximity effect, and gate-tunable Josephson coupling through a graphene
  weak link.
- Superconductivity intrinsic to magic-angle TBG (Cao *et al.*, *Nature* 556, 43
  (2018)), and how it differs from proximity-induced superconductivity.

## 8.4 Planned models, in order

1. A continuum-model band structure for TBG, validated against the untwisted
   (single-layer and AB-bilayer) limits.
2. A normal-metal–graphene–superconductor conductance model (Bogoliubov–de Gennes
   scattering), validated against the known single-layer graphene result.
3. A TBG–superconductor junction built from 1 and 2.
4. The contact physics of Chapter 4 applied to the superconducting electrode.

## 8.5 Exact limits each model must reproduce

- Twist angle → 0 and large-angle decoupling limits of the TBG band structure.
- Normal-state conductance when the superconducting gap is set to zero.
- The known Andreev-reflection limits for single-layer graphene.

## 8.6 Open questions to settle before drafting

- Scope: proximity-induced or intrinsic superconductivity, or both.
- Which experimental data set to benchmark against.
- Which of the single-layer contact results survive a superconducting electrode.
