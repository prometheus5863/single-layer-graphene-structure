SECTION 1.  (4.29) IS NOT A LINEARISATION OF (4.28)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Chapter 4 says so in three places.  It is checkable, and false.
  A linearisation of (4.28) about dV = 0 would share its leading
  order there.  (4.28) has a*n + b*sqrt(n) = dV with the sqrt term
  dominating as dV -> 0, so n ~ (dV/b)^2 -- QUADRATIC.  (4.29) has
  C_q(0) finite, so n ~ (C_ox/e)*f(C_q(0))*dV -- LINEAR.

  The two terms of (4.28) are equal at dV = 2*b^2/a = 6.518863e-03 V
  (6.519 mV), so the quadratic regime is dV << 6.52 mV.  THE FIRST
  DRAFT OF THIS CHECK SWEPT dV DOWN TO 1e-4 V ONLY and measured an
  exponent of 1.9438, failing a +-0.01 criterion.  It did not
  measure the exponent: 1e-4 V is 65x below the crossover, where
  the correction is O(sqrt(dV/dV_x)) ~ 12 %%, so it measured the
  WIDTH OF THE CROSSOVER.  The repair is to evaluate the limit
  where the limit is, and to print the crossover drive so the
  criterion is derived from the model instead of guessed
  (2026-10-09: find out what a failed check measured).

  What (4.29) actually is.  Differentiating (4.28):
    d(dV)/dn = e/C_ox + e/C_Q(n),  C_Q(n) = e^2 dn/dE_F = 2e*sqrt(n)/A_F
    =>  dn/d(dV) = (C_ox/e)*C_Q(n)/(C_Q(n) + C_ox)             (R2)
  (4.29) is (R2) with n/dV written for dn/d(dV): THE EXACT
  DIFFERENTIAL RELATION USED AS AN ALGEBRAIC ONE, i.e. a one-point
  rectangle rule for the integral of (R2).  That is a quadrature
  error, and a rectangle rule is worst where the slope varies
  fastest -- near the Dirac point, which is exactly where the
  2026-10-08 raw disagreement reached +68 %.

  Literature position, checked today (live search available).
  Pasadas/Jimenez-lineage compact-model review arXiv:2209.00388
  writes the gate-to-channel relation as implicit in V_c (the
  chemical-potential shift) and solves it NUMERICALLY in the
  simulator; its series-capacitance expression appears as
  dV/dV_c = 1 + C_q(V_c)/(C_t + C_b), a DIFFERENTIAL relation,
  and its C_q is evaluated at V_c and never at the overdrive.
  Both of this section's points are therefore the literature's
  own conventions, not a new convention introduced here.  It also
  states the Fermi-Dirac form "is not convenient for a compact
  model" and replaces C_q by k*c1*sqrt(1 + (V_c/c1)^2), whose
  c1 -> 0 limit is exactly (R1).  So (4.28) is the T = 0 limit of
  a standard literature approximation -- which is also the
  limitation stated in Section 6 below.
