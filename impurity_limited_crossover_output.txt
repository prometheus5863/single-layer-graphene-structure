SECTION 9.  The design rule, which is the device statement
  N_imp* = C*C_ox*V/(e*A) -- the dirtiest channel a gate stack can
  drive past the crossover at a given overdrive:
  gate stack and overdrive           N_imp* (e12 cm^-2) vs. actual      
  10 nm HfO2 at 1 V                  5.757            4.61x the reference
  90 nm SiO2 at 5 V                  0.499            0.40x the reference
  [PASS] the HfO2 deposition budget is REAL but thin: the paper's dirty end sits just inside it
         a 10 nm HfO2 gate driven to 1 V tolerates dN_imp <= 4.507e12 cm^-2 of deposition damage; the paper reports up to 4.00e12, so the headroom is 12.7 %.  This is the falsifiable form of 10-30's "a thicker or dirtier dielectric could eat it".
  [PASS] the back-gated device cannot reach n_c at ANY cleanliness it plausibly has -- and the reason is the inverted one
         the 90 nm stack at 5 V would need N_imp <= 0.499e12 cm^-2, i.e. the channel 2.51x CLEANER than it is.  Cleaning a channel moves n_c DOWN, so better material reaches the asymmetry-killing regime at LOWER gate voltage -- the opposite of the intuition that a cleaner device is the more ideal one in every respect.

SECTION 10. D10: the source's two numbers constrain each other
  [PASS] arXiv:1010.0913's mobility loss and its added-impurity range are mutually consistent only at the CLEAN end of its own range
         its "~50 % of the mobility" implies dN_imp = 1.250e12 cm^-2 given C, which is 0.83x its stated low end (1.5e12) and 0.31x its stated high end (4.0e12).  So the two numbers agree to 17 % at the clean end and disagree by a factor of 3.2 at the dirty end.  THE ESCAPE IS REAL AND IS NOT ASSUMED AWAY: if HfO2 deposition also adds NEUTRAL defects then A grows too, the mobility loss is shared between the channels, and a larger dN_imp becomes consistent.  That would change A, which this module holds fixed, and it is logged as an open item rather than modelled here.
