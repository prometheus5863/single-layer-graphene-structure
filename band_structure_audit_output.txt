RESULT 2 -- why it survived: the DOS figure cannot see the bands
======================================================================
  calculate_density_of_states() returns |E|/(pi*2.8^2)
  analytically.  MUTATION TEST -- replace the Hamiltonian with a
  k-independent gapped matrix diag(+7, -7), which has no Dirac
  point and no dispersion at all, and recompute the DOS:
    shipped DOS identical before and after: True
    -> the shipped DOS figure has ZERO sensitivity to the
       Hamiltonian.  It would print the correct V shape for any
       band structure whatsoever, including the broken one.

  A DOS computed FROM the bands is sensitive, and is the check
  that was missing.  Computed here over the reciprocal cell:
    van Hove peak on the electron side at E = 2.771 eV
    exact tight-binding value                  = 2.800 eV (= t)
    relative error 0.010  (bin width 0.067 eV)
    DOS in the bin straddling E = 0: 5.080e+16 states/(eV m^2),
      which is 0.4% of the van Hove peak and is consistent
      with a linear DOS averaged over one bin: the derived value
      is (slope/4) x bin width = 2.986e+16, ratio 1.70

  THE PATTERN, and it is the sharpest instance in five weeks:
  three band/DOS figures in this repository, and not one of them
  was a calculation that could have failed.  Two draw the known
  answer (E = +/- v_F|k|, |E|) and the third computes and is
  wrong.  An unfalsifiable figure is worse than no figure,
  because it certifies the thing beside it.

RESULT 3 (NEW) -- the validity range of the linear cone that
                  Chapters 4-7 import
======================================================================
  Carrier density from the FULL tight-binding bands against
  n = E_F^2/(pi hbar^2 v_F^2).  Both spins and both valleys.
    E_F (eV)      n_TB (cm^-2)   n_Dirac (cm^-2)     ratio
        0.05        2.2614e+11        2.2364e+11    1.0112
        0.10        8.9044e+11        8.9455e+11    0.9954
        0.20        3.5712e+12        3.5782e+12    0.9980
        0.30        8.0682e+12        8.0509e+12    1.0021
        0.50        2.2478e+13        2.2364e+13    1.0051
        0.80        5.8084e+13        5.7251e+13    1.0145
        1.00        9.1423e+13        8.9455e+13    1.0220
        1.50        2.1213e+14        2.0127e+14    1.0539

  The linear form is good to 1% over 0.1 <= E_F <= 0.5 eV and to
  5% up to E_F = 1.0 eV.  Stated as a WINDOW and not as an upper
  limit, because the deviation is NOT monotone: at E_F = 0.05 eV
  the ratio is 1.0112, worse than at 0.5 eV.  That is a
  k-sampling artefact and not physics -- at 0.05 eV the cone
  occupies 5.9e-05 of the cell area, so an 1800x1800 grid places only
  ~192 samples inside it.  Reported rather than smoothed over: the
  clean physical trend is the monotone rise above 0.2 eV.
  Every gate bias in Chapter 4 and every photon energy in
  Chapter 6 sits inside the 1% window, so the approximation those
  chapters make is quantified rather than assumed -- for the
  first time in this repository.
  Sign of the deviation: n_TB EXCEEDS n_Dirac above ~0.25 eV
  (2.20% at 1.0 eV), because trigonal warping flattens the band
  and adds states.  A device model using the linear DOS there
  UNDERSTATES the carrier density, i.e. errs conservatively for
  drive current and against itself for quantum capacitance.
