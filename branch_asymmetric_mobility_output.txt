  Why this is the discriminator rather than a fit convenience:
  the contact term enters R_odd with NO factor of L, so it cannot be
  absorbed into the slope at any length, and the channel term enters
  with L and cannot be absorbed into the intercept.  Huard et al.
  (arXiv:0804.2040) report exactly this: their odd resistance
  collapses when scaled by the metal/graphene INTERFACE WIDTH, and the
  bulk-only reading of the same data gives a resistivity asymmetry
  that drifts with probe spacing -- the signature of a nonzero
  intercept being misread as a slope.

  Cost of the simplification: a single-length measurement cannot do
  this at all.  At the shipped L = 200 nm the contact-only and
  channel-only scenarios differ in R_odd by only:
    channel-only R_odd =    80.9278 Ohm
    contact-only R_odd =   120.0000 Ohm
    ratio              =     0.6744
  i.e. at 200 nm the two mechanisms are within a factor 1.48 of each
  other and a single-length experiment is blind between them.

SECTION 6.  What the shipped model is, in the new vocabulary
  The committed GFET model sits exactly on the mu_e == mu_h diagonal,
  i.e. r = 0, so every number already in this repository is the r = 0
  case of the forms above and NONE of them moves.  What changes is
  what they are claims ABOUT: the floor e*mu*n_p is a geometric mean
  evaluated on a diagonal, and the V_dirac = 0.80 V offset is a
  doping offset only under an assumption (r = 0) that the model makes
  silently and that no committed check tests.
