# I1 — RESULT 2026-11-20: CNP shift vs channel length, compared with Nouchi 2011

**Source:** R. Nouchi, T. Saito, K. Tanigaki, arXiv:[1102.2954](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.2954) (2011). The journal reference was not read from the fetched text; it is believed to be Appl. Phys. Express 4, 035101 and needs confirming. The paper has two-terminal devices with exfoliated SLG on 300 nm SiO₂, channel lengths L = 0.4–2.5 µm, and contacts of Au (on <1 nm Cr), Cu and Ag. The figures were images only. The usable data, in `nouchi2011_vnp_vs_L.csv`, are as follows:
- **Au:** V_NP moves from −43 V at L = 2.5 µm to −71 V at L = 0.4 µm, so the contact is n-doping.
- **Cu, Ag:** V_NP moves positive as L shortens, so these contacts are p-doping. Only the sign is given.

The authors note that these signs are the reverse of the simple work-function expectation.

**Why this test:** a doping tail from the contact moves the charge-neutrality gate voltage as L shrinks, and the direction of that move gives the contact's doping sign. Unlike the 11-19b Passi check, the model can fail this test.

**Run:** `run_nouchi_vnp_shift.py`, with src unchanged. It uses calc (111) W from Khomyakov 2009 (I2) with W0 = 5.4 eV, λ = 250 nm and C_ox for 300 nm SiO₂. V_NP is taken where the two-terminal R(V_g) peaks, and each shift is measured from the L = 2.5 µm value.
- **Model fix found on the way:** I1's superposition S = f(x)+f(L−x) goes above 1 mid-channel when L ≲ 2λ, and the gate's leverage there then changes sign. On a first pass that made all three metals appear to "agree". The main model now uses the bounded S = f₁+f₂−f₁f₂ instead, and the transcript shows the plain-sum result in brackets.
- **Effect on 11-19:** the TLM windows there start at L ≥ 0.5 µm with n_b fixed, where S ≤ 1. The 11-19 results should therefore be unaffected, but this has not been rechecked.

**Validation (exact limit):** with n_c = 0, V_NP − V_0 = 0 at every L (|Δ| < 2×10⁻¹³ V). It passes.

| metal | model n_c (cm⁻²) | model ΔV_NP, 0.4 vs 2.5 µm | Nouchi sign | match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Au | −1.25×10¹² (p) | +74 V | − (−28 V) | **no** |
| Cu | +1.78×10¹² (n) | −102 V | + | **no** |
| Ag | +6.19×10¹² (n) | −257 V | + | **no** |

**Inverse fit for Au:** reproducing Nouchi's −28 V needs n_c = +4.5×10¹¹ cm⁻² (n-type). That corresponds to W = 5.33 eV in the src model, close to the experimental polycrystalline W of 5.31 eV in Khomyakov's table. However, I2 showed that W and W0 must come from one source, so this agreement may be a coincidence. With n_c fixed by the endpoint, the model curve is ΔV = −28, −15, −9, −6, −3, −1 and 0 V at L = 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 2.5 µm. Nouchi's intermediate points are in images only, so this shape is a prediction that has not been tested.

**What came out**
1. **0/3 doping signs match.** The model's signs are reversed for Au, Cu and Ag on this dataset.
2. **The disagreement is not clean evidence against the model.** STS on Au finds p-type doping (Sławińska 2012, used in I2), and DFT agrees, so Nouchi's n-type Au disagrees with other experiments. Nouchi's Au sits on a Cr adhesion layer, and Cr is a low-W chemisorbing metal. The Cu and Ag films were Au-capped and may have oxidised. So this is a sample-specific sign test, and the model failed it.
3. **The magnitude is in range.** One n_c of a few ×10¹¹ cm⁻² reproduces the Au shift, so the λ = 250 nm tail length is consistent with Nouchi's L dependence. The tail length is not ruled out; only the sign the src model assigns from W is.

**Metric:** I1 now has a data comparison with a real pass/fail outcome, and it failed on sign. It fails on sign for this dataset only. The tail-length part is consistent. The I1 kill condition (TLM absorbs < 10 % of R_extra) is a different question and is not hit. I1 stays running and is not PAPER-READY.

**Next experiment:** obtain V_NP(L) data for clean, adhesion-free contacts that can be digitised. One candidate is the Au data in arXiv:1108.3238 ("Electronic Properties of Clean Au-Graphene Contacts"; authors not yet read), still to be checked. That would separate the model's sign error from Nouchi's Cr and oxide confounders. If clean-contact data also shows the opposite sign, the W → n_c sign rule in src is wrong for these metals, which would be a finding for Harsh to bring to the thesis.
