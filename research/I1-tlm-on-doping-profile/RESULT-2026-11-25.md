- **Huard et al., PRB 78, 121402 (2008)**, [arXiv:0804.2040](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.2040). This measures exactly this odd part, 2R_odd·w = R(n) − R(−n) at invasive Ti/Au and Pd probes. - **Nagashio et al.**, [arXiv:1004.2074](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2074). The contacts are Cr/Au, and R_c is given per device, not per branch. The paper reports ρ_e/ρ_h within ±0.1 and argues that p–n formation at Cr/Au is negligible. It cannot be used to form R_c(n) − R_c(p).

**Validation (exact limit):** with C_I → 10⁻¹² F/m² the profile is flat on both branches, so ΔR should be 0 exactly. The run gives ΔR = 1.25×10⁻⁸ Ω·µm, which passes.

**What came out** (Ω·µm per contact; all TLM fits have R² ≥ 0.9988)

| \|n\| (10¹² cm⁻²) | 0.5 | 1.2 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ΔR model | 898 | 739 | 417 | 237 | 109 | 63 | 49 |
| Huard Eq. 3 sharp p–n bound | 188 | 121 | 94 | 77 | 60 | 50 | 47 |

1. **Sign, H1: matches.** ΔR > 0 everywhere, so the electron side is more resistive. Huard observe R_odd > 0 for Ti/Au and Pd and attribute it to p-type n_c.
2. **Density dependence, H2: does not match.** Over 1.2–7 ×10¹² cm⁻², the range where Huard state that R_odd "saturates to a finite value", the model falls 11.7× (log slope −1.42). The cause is the gate-tuned edge: on the n-branch the gate pushes the Au contact toward neutrality, and the p–n junction closes at n_g* = C_I·0.59 eV/e = 9.3×10¹² cm⁻². A saturating R_odd needs n_c that stays well away from zero over this range, meaning stronger pinning under the metal (a smaller gate lever on n_c) than src's C_q-in-series-with-C_I contact gives. The caveat is large: Huard's stack is Ti(3–5 nm)/Au, so W − W0 and the pinning may differ from clean Au, and the claim comes from the text, not digitised points.
3. **Ballistic floor, H3: the diffusive model approaches it.** ΔR/bound falls from 6.3 to 1.05 at 8×10¹². Near the closing density the diffusive junction term is no larger than the ballistic resistance of an ideal sharp p–n interface, and a smooth (λ = 27 nm) junction should lie above that bound. This is direct support for I6's wedge, that src's diffusive R_extra underestimates the junction at high n.

**Metric:** first data comparison for the surviving I1 claim. The sign is confirmed, but the density dependence disagrees with the only text-level data available. The p/n-asymmetry prediction in its current form (≈ +420 Ω·µm at 2×10¹², decaying as n^−1.4) is therefore **not supported**.
