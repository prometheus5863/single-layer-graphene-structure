# I1 — RESULT 2026-11-24: on the data-fitted profile, TLM measures the junction faithfully; the 11-19 curvature/window artefact disappears

**Run:** `run_tlm_gate_tuned.py`, with src unchanged. It repeats the 11-19 TLM extraction (`run_tlm_extraction.py`) on the 11-23 gate-tuned contact edge, using Au W − W0 = 0.59 eV and λ = 27 nm. Both values were fitted to Malec & Davidović 2011 (arXiv:[1108.3238](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.3238)) on 11-23. The edge equation and the two-contact S_or are **copied** from `run_gate_tuned_edge.py`, not imported, because importing that script re-runs it and overwrites the 11-23 outputs. Output files: `tlm_gate_tuned_transcript.txt`, `tlm_gate_tuned_results.csv` and `tlm_gate_tuned.png`.

**Assumption:** the gate-induced density n_g is set equal to the channel density n_bulk, so densities are counted from the channel's own neutrality point. Passi et al. 2018 (arXiv:[1807.04772](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04772), [doi:10.1002/admi.201801285](https://doi.org/10.1002/admi.201801285)) does not give the oxide thickness. The −40 V p-branch is therefore bracketed with n = −2 and −5 ×10¹² cm⁻², as on 11-19b. The n-branch uses +2 and +5 ×10¹² cm⁻². R_int is 0.

**Validation (exact limit):** with C_I → 10⁻¹² F/m² the metal is decoupled, so the profile is flat. The fit gives R_c,TLM = 1.7×10⁻⁸ Ω·µm (exact value 0) and a slope of 756.894152 Ω/sq, equal to R_sheet. It passes.

For contrast, the same table at the src λ = 250 nm gives TLM/R_extra = 0.70–1.31. The n-low case there has R² = 0.92–0.99, a residual of 2.1 kΩ·µm, and a sign flip in the 0.5–5 µm window. This reproduces the 11-19 artefact.

1. **Kill condition: not hit.** R_c,TLM picks up 99–111 % of R_extra on both branches, far above the 10 % threshold. This covers Au only. The other physisorbed metals have not been refitted on the gate-tuned edge, but a single metal at ≥ 10 % already means the condition is not hit.
2. **The 11-19 candidate claim does not survive the data-fitted profile.** That claim was that a δn ∝ 1/x tail makes the TLM R_c window-dependent and bends R_tot(L). At λ = 27 nm the window spread is at most 1.8× (p-low, 80–144 Ω·µm) and about 1.1–1.2× elsewhere, compared with 2–3× before. The R² values are all ≥ 0.9996, so the curvature would be invisible in any published TLM (Passi reports R² > 0.984). The artefact only existed because src's λ = 250 nm was too long. **I1's "TLM artefact" headline is therefore withdrawn.** TLM is a faithful meter of the junction term on this profile.
3. **What does survive is the branch asymmetry of the junction term.** At |n| = 2×10¹² cm⁻² it is −112 Ω·µm on the p-branch and +306 Ω·µm on the n-branch, a difference of about 420 Ω·µm. On the n-branch, Au's contact stays p-type until n_g* = C_I·0.59 eV/e² ≈ 9.3×10¹² cm⁻², so it forms a p–n junction. This is a sign-definite, testable prediction for any ambipolar Au R_c(V_g) measured by TLM.
4. **Passi −40 V:** the junction term is negative (−16 to −144 Ω·µm), because the p-doped contact region conducts better than the p-channel. On this model all of Passi's 519 Ω·µm is therefore interface or transmission resistance, about 535–660 Ω·µm. On 11-19b, at λ = 250 nm, the model attributed 24–55 % to the junction. Linear fits remain consistent with R² > 0.984.

**Metric:** the kill condition was retested and is not hit. The idea's distinguishing claim has changed. The TLM-artefact claim is withdrawn, and the remaining prediction is the p/n asymmetry of R_c,TLM(V_g) for Au. That prediction has not yet been compared against data. Not PAPER-READY.
