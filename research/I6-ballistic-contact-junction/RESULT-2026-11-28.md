# I6 — RESULT 2026-11-28: diffusive vs ballistic junction term, head to head (experiment 3)

**Script:** `run_diffusive_head_to_head.py`. **Outputs:** `diffusive_head_to_head_results.csv`, `diffusive_head_to_head_transcript.txt`, `diffusive_head_to_head.png`. src is imported, not edited. `run_full_rc.py` (11-27) is imported; it re-ran once and rewrote its outputs byte-identically (git shows no diff).

## What was run
- The 11-27 one-parameter procedure, R_c = R_mg(n_c) + junction term, run twice:
  - **ballistic:** R_J = R_Landauer − R_Sharvin (11-26 k_y-resolved Dirac transfer matrix);
  - **diffusive:** src-style R_extra = ∫[1/σ(n(x)) − 1/σ(n_b)]dx (11-26 definition, src's puddle-regularised σ).
- ρ_c was fixed to Passi 2018 at −40 V (519 Ω·µm) **separately for each model and each λ** (5, 15, 27 nm), at L_c = 0.5, 1, 10 µm and fit density n_g = −2 and −5 ×10¹² cm⁻². (11-27 had reused the λ = 27 nm ρ_c for all λ in the Malec sweep; refitting gives both models the same freedom.)
- Two no-refit predictions per fit: Passi R_c(CNP)/R_c(−40 V) (measured 1372/519 = 2.64; `../I1-tlm-on-doping-profile/passi2018_au_tlm.csv`, Passi et al., doi:[10.1002/admi.201801285](https://doi.org/10.1002/admi.201801285)) and Malec 2011 R_c(0 V)/R_c(−100 V) (measured 128/95 = 1.35, text values as used on 11-27). CNP = mean of the n_g = ±n_puddle sides.

## Validation (one, exact limit)
Flat profile n_c = n_b = −2×10¹² cm⁻²: diffusive R_extra = 0 exactly, and R_c equals the closed-form TLM √(ρ_c R_s)coth(L_c/L_T): 277.3697 vs 277.3697 Ω·µm (relative error 6×10⁻⁸). **PASS.**

## What came out
Junction terms (Ω·µm) at the densities that drive the two ratios:

|---|---|---|---|

| 15 nm | 0.1 / −109 | 0.5 / −667 | 638 / 569 |

Predicted ratios (range over L_c and fit density):

| model | λ | Passi CNP/−40 V (meas. 2.64) | Malec 0/−100 V (meas. 1.35) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ballistic | 5 nm | 1.40–1.69 | 1.44–1.95 |
| ballistic | 15 nm | 1.64–1.92 | 1.67–2.25 |
| ballistic | 27 nm | 1.80–2.09 | 1.84–2.46 |
| diffusive | 5 nm | 1.03–1.30 | 1.03–1.43 |
| diffusive | 15 nm | 0.98–1.28 | 0.97–1.37 |
| diffusive | 27 nm | 0.92–1.36 | 0.90–1.31 |

1. **The two datasets disagree on which model wins.**
   - Passi: ballistic is closer at every λ (best 2.09 vs 2.64, −21 %); diffusive gives 0.92–1.36, i.e. almost no Dirac-point rise (−49 % to −65 %).
   - Malec: diffusive brackets the measured 1.35 (1.03–1.43); ballistic overshoots at every λ (best 1.44 at λ = 5 nm, +7 %; typical +10 % to +80 %).
   - A combined score (RMS of the two mean log-errors, in the transcript) favours ballistic at all λ (0.54–0.61 vs 0.87–0.89), but only because Passi's miss is larger. That is a weighting choice, not a verdict.
2. **Why diffusive flattens the Dirac-point rise:** on the p side the src R_extra is negative (the Au-doped tail conducts better than the channel), and at the CNP p-side it reaches −282 to −1115 Ω·µm, cancelling most of the n-side term. Landauer cannot give a negative junction term (channel Sharvin is the bottleneck), so this cancellation is a property of the diffusive formula, not of the data.
3. **Neither model reaches Passi's 2.64** with one parameter. Both under-predict; the gap is larger than the λ and L_c spread.
4. ρ_c is nearly model-independent (2.4×10⁻⁶–2.1×10⁻⁵ Ω·cm² over all fits, same short-contact ρ_c–L_c degeneracy as 11-27).

## Success metric
- Part 1 (R_bal and R_extra differ > 2× at λ = 5–15 nm): met on 11-26, and today again in sign on the p side.
- Part 2 (ballistic closer to measured Au R_c): **split, not met.** Ballistic wins on Passi, diffusive wins on Malec. Two text-value ratios from different interfaces cannot settle it.
- Kill condition (model choice does not matter): **not hit** — the models give opposite Dirac-point behaviour.
